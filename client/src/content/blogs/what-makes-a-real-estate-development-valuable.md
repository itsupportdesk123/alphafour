When people talk about the value of a property, they usually mean its price. Price matters, but it is only a snapshot of what someone is willing to pay at one moment. The more useful question for a homebuyer, investor or business owner is what makes a development worth owning, living in or working from for many years, and what makes it attractive to the next buyer or tenant.

The answer is rarely one thing. Lasting value comes from a combination of qualities, some visible on a site visit and some found only in documents, drawings and the way a building is run. Here are the ones that matter most, and how to assess them.

## Location and connectivity

Location remains the starting point, but it is more than an address. What matters is how easily people can reach the places they need: workplaces, schools, hospitals, shops and public transport.

In Chennai, that might mean being close to a station on the operational Blue and Green lines of the metro, having good access to a growth corridor such as OMR or GST Road, or sitting in an established district with mature roads and services. Be careful to separate what exists today from what is planned. A location that already works is a stronger foundation than one that depends on future projects.

Look closely at the immediate surroundings too. The width and condition of the approach road, how the street drains in heavy rain, noise levels and what could be built on neighbouring plots all affect daily life and future appeal.

## Clear title and approvals

A development is only as secure as its paperwork. Clear title means the land has a clean, traceable history of ownership. In Tamil Nadu, buyers typically review the parent documents and chain of title, the patta naming the land owner, and the encumbrance certificate (EC), which shows registered transactions on the property. Many of these records can be checked through the state's online portals.

Approvals matter just as much. Building plans need planning permission from the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) or the local body, depending on the building, and outside the CMDA area layouts are approved by the Directorate of Town and Country Planning (DTCP). Projects above the thresholds in the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 must be registered with the Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TNRERA) before they are advertised or sold.

Because documents vary from property to property, it is wise to have them reviewed by a property lawyer. Our [due-diligence checklist for buying property in Chennai](/blogs/buying-property-in-chennai-checklist) walks through each step.

## Design, light and everyday comfort

Good design is felt every day. Homes and workspaces with plenty of natural light, cross-ventilation and sensible layouts are more pleasant to use and less expensive to run, because they rely less on artificial lighting and air conditioning.

![An open living room with glass walls and an indoor garden](openLiving "An open living room with glass walls and an indoor garden")

When you visit, pay attention to:

- how much daylight reaches each room at different times of day
- whether windows on opposite sides allow air to move through
- how much of the floor area is genuinely usable, rather than lost to awkward corners and long corridors
- storage, and whether your furniture will fit comfortably
- privacy from neighbouring buildings
- shading on windows that face the afternoon sun

Many buyers in Chennai also consider Vastu when choosing a home, and a well-planned project will make the orientation of rooms and entrances clear in its drawings.

## Construction quality that lasts

Construction quality determines how a building ages. A well-built structure stays sound and dry with routine maintenance; a poorly built one demands constant repairs.

Chennai's conditions make this especially important. Its hot, humid coastal climate and salt-laden sea air speed up corrosion, and IS 456:2000, the Indian code of practice for reinforced concrete, treats concrete exposed to a coastal environment as a severe exposure condition, calling for better concrete quality and more cover over the reinforcement. Waterproofing must cope with the northeast monsoon, and because the city lies in Seismic Zone III, structural design must follow the earthquake-resistant design standard, IS 1893.

![A multi-storey building frame under construction](buildingFrame "A multi-storey building frame under construction")

Buyers cannot inspect every column, but they can ask sensible questions. Who is the structural engineer? Which codes has the design followed? How is waterproofing handled on the roof, in wet areas and below ground? Which brands and specifications are committed in the agreement? Our article on [the construction lifecycle](/blogs/construction-lifecycle-foundation-to-finishing) explains what happens at each stage.

> A building's real quality shows years after handover, in everything that has not needed fixing.

## Maintenance, management and amenities that get used

The value of a development depends heavily on how it is looked after. Lifts, pumps, generators, fire systems and common areas all need regular maintenance, and the arrangements for this should be clear before you buy. Ask who will manage the building after handover, how maintenance charges will be set and how the owners' association will be formed.

Amenities deserve a practical eye. A long list of facilities can look attractive in a brochure, but each one has to be maintained and paid for. The amenities that add lasting value are usually the ones people use regularly: adequate parking, reliable power backup and water supply, secure entrances, well-kept landscaped areas, and spaces suited to the people who will actually live or work there.

## Developer reliability, and how to check it

A development is a promise made over several years, so the reliability of the developer matters. You can assess it without relying on marketing material:

1. Check the project's registration and the details published on TNRERA's website.
2. Visit completed projects by the same developer and, where possible, speak to the people who live or work there.
3. Compare what was promised in earlier projects with what was delivered.
4. Notice how clearly the developer answers questions about approvals, specifications and timelines.
5. Read the agreement for sale carefully, with legal advice, before paying significant amounts.

The law also gives buyers certain protections. Under the Act, for example, the promoter is liable to fix structural defects or workmanship defects brought to their notice within five years of handing over possession.

## Value that holds over time

Resale and rental appeal follow naturally from everything above. A well-located property with clean title, sound construction, good light and responsible management is easier to sell or let, because the next buyer or tenant will be asking the same questions you are asking now.

The most valuable developments are rarely the ones with the loudest launches. They are the ones that are still well regarded long after the last unit has been handed over, because their location, paperwork, design and upkeep have all stood the test of time. If you are weighing up a property, take your time, check the details and ask direct questions. A good developer will welcome them.
