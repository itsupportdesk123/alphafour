A finished building hides most of the work that went into it. Behind the paint and tiles lie months of planning, testing, excavation, concrete, steel, cabling and pipework, with each stage depending on the one before. Understanding that sequence helps buyers ask better questions, read progress updates with more confidence and know what to look for at handover.

This guide walks through the main stages of a typical building project, with notes on the conditions that make construction in Chennai distinctive.

## Feasibility and soil investigation

Every project starts with questions about the land. Is the title clear? What do the planning rules allow on this plot? What will the building need to serve its users, and can the site support it?

A soil investigation, carried out by drilling boreholes and testing samples, tells the engineers what lies beneath the surface: the type and strength of the soil, the depth to firmer strata and the level of the groundwater. Ground conditions vary considerably across Chennai, and in many parts of the city groundwater can be close to the surface, particularly after the monsoon. These findings shape the choice of foundation and the approach to excavation, so a thorough investigation at this stage avoids expensive surprises later.

## Design and approvals

With the site understood, the design team develops the architectural, structural and services designs together. The structural design must follow the relevant Indian standards, including IS 456:2000 for reinforced concrete and IS 1893 for earthquake-resistant design, since Chennai is in Seismic Zone III. The National Building Code of India 2016 guides structural safety, fire and life safety, and building services.

Approvals follow. Building plans need planning permission from the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) or the local body, such as the Greater Chennai Corporation, depending on the building, within the framework of the Tamil Nadu Combined Development and Building Rules, 2019. Projects above the thresholds in the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 must also be registered with the Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TNRERA) before they are advertised or sold.

## Excavation and foundations

Construction proper begins below ground. Excavation for basements or foundations must be planned to keep the sides stable and to protect neighbouring buildings. Where groundwater is high, dewatering keeps the excavation dry enough to work in safely.

![Excavators and a bulldozer working in a large excavation](earthworks "Excavators and a bulldozer at work in a large excavation")

The foundation type depends on the soil report and the loads the building will carry. A building may stand on shallow foundations, such as individual footings or a raft spread beneath the whole structure, or on piles that carry its loads down to stronger ground. Below-ground concrete in a coastal city needs particular care: IS 456:2000 treats concrete exposed to a coastal environment as a severe exposure condition, which calls for better concrete quality and more cover over the reinforcement. Waterproofing of basements and retaining walls is best done properly the first time, because it is very difficult to put right later.

Scheduling matters too. Chennai gets most of its rainfall from the northeast monsoon, roughly October to December, and well-run projects aim to complete deep excavation and below-ground work outside that period wherever possible.

## The superstructure

Once the foundations are complete, the frame rises floor by floor: columns, beams, slabs, shear walls, lift cores and staircases. Each pour of concrete is preceded by careful checks of the formwork, the reinforcement and any embedded services, and is followed by curing.

![Workers on scaffolding fixing reinforcement for concrete columns](columnRebar "Workers on scaffolding fixing reinforcement for concrete columns")

Curing, which means keeping concrete moist for days after casting so that it gains strength properly, is especially important in Chennai's heat, where exposed surfaces can dry out quickly. Inadequate curing can lead to weaker concrete and cracking. On a well-run site, you will see slabs kept wet or covered, and test samples taken from pours so that the strength of the concrete can be checked in a laboratory.

> Much of what makes a building durable is decided by work that will never be seen again once it is covered.

## Services: mechanical, electrical and plumbing

As the frame progresses, the mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) services follow. These include electrical wiring and panels, water supply and drainage, fire detection and firefighting systems, lifts, air conditioning, power backup and, in Tamil Nadu, rainwater harvesting structures, which have been mandatory for buildings since 2003.

Good coordination between trades is essential here. Openings in slabs and walls for pipes and ducts should be planned before concrete is poured rather than cut afterwards. Pumps, panels and generators are best located where floodwater cannot easily reach them, a lesson reinforced by the severe flooding Chennai experienced in late 2015 and again during Cyclone Michaung in December 2023.

## Finishes, testing and handover

Finishing work brings the building to life: plastering, waterproofing of wet areas and terraces, flooring, doors and windows, painting, façade work, fixtures and landscaping. Near the coast, finishes and fittings should be chosen with salt-laden sea air in mind, because it speeds up the corrosion of exposed steel and fittings.

Before handover, systems are tested and commissioned, including water-tightness, pumps, lifts, fire systems, electrical installations and drainage. A completion or occupancy certificate is issued by the authority concerned once it is satisfied that the building has been built in line with the approved plans. Buyers should check which certificate applies to their property and verify it with that authority.

After handover comes the defect liability period. Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, the promoter is liable to fix structural defects or workmanship defects brought to their notice within five years of handing over possession. ## What this means for buyers

Knowing the lifecycle helps you follow a project with more confidence. As a buyer, it is reasonable to:

- ask what the soil investigation found and what type of foundation was chosen
- check the project's approvals and its TNRERA registration
- ask how the construction programme allows for the monsoon
- look for signs of good practice on site visits, such as proper curing and neat reinforcement work
- request a clear handover checklist, along with documentation for systems and warranties
