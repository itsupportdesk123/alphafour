Buying a home or commercial space is one of the largest decisions most people make, and in Chennai, as anywhere, the paperwork matters as much as the floor plan. A beautiful apartment is a poor purchase if the title is unclear, the approvals do not match the building, or the agreement leaves the buyer exposed.

This checklist sets out the main checks to work through before committing to a property in Chennai, roughly in the order you will meet them. It is a practical guide, not legal advice. Rules and procedures change and every property is different, so verify each point with the authority concerned and have a property lawyer review the documents before you sign or pay.

## Start with TNRERA registration

For a new project, begin with the Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TNRERA). Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, projects above the thresholds in the Act must be registered before they are advertised or sold, and promoters must keep project details and registration information on the authority's website.

1. Ask the developer for the project's registration details
2. Find the project in the registration records on TNRERA's official website
3. Check that the project name, promoter and location match what you have been told
4. Compare the details the promoter has provided with the brochure and the sales conversation
5. If a project that should be registered is not, treat that as a serious warning sign

Our guide to [RERA in Tamil Nadu](/blogs/rera-tamil-nadu-homebuyers) explains the Act's main protections in more detail.

## Trace the title

Clear title is the foundation of any purchase. In Tamil Nadu, three sets of records sit at the heart of the title check:

- Parent documents: the earlier sale deeds and related documents that form the chain of title, showing how the property passed from one owner to the next
- Patta: the revenue record that names the owner of the land
- Encumbrance certificate (EC): issued by the Registration Department, showing the registered transactions on a property, such as sales and mortgages

Your lawyer will want to see a continuous chain of title with no unexplained gaps, going back as far as they consider necessary. The patta should be consistent with the title documents, and the EC should not show outstanding mortgages or conflicting transactions. For an apartment, ask how the land, and your undivided share in it, will be conveyed to you.

Many records, including the EC, can be checked through the state's online portals, which makes a useful first step. Bear in mind that an EC reflects registered transactions only, so it is one important part of the picture rather than the whole of it.

## Confirm the approvals match the building

Every building needs planning permission. Within the Chennai Metropolitan Area, that comes from the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) or the local body, such as the Greater Chennai Corporation, depending on the building. Outside the CMDA area, layouts are approved by the Directorate of Town and Country Planning (DTCP). Common building rules across the state are set by the Tamil Nadu Combined Development and Building Rules, 2019.

1. Ask for copies of the planning permission and the approved plans
2. Check that the approval covers the use you intend, whether residential or commercial
3. Compare the approved plan with what is being built: the number of floors, setbacks, parking and the layout of your unit
4. For a plot, confirm that the layout itself has been approved by the appropriate authority

A building that departs from its approved plan can cause problems later, including at completion, when arranging a loan or at resale. The building permit is a promise about what will be built; your site visits are how you check that the promise is being kept.

## Understand the money and the measurements

Several financial and contractual details deserve close attention before you sign:

1. Check the guideline value, the government's value used for registration, on the state's online portals, and confirm the charges payable at registration with the Registration Department or your lawyer
2. Make sure the agreement states the carpet area, which under RERA means the net usable floor area, excluding external walls, service shafts, exclusive balconies or verandahs and open terraces, but including internal partition walls
3. If other area figures appear in the brochure or agreement, ask exactly what each one includes
4. Remember that, under RERA, a promoter cannot take more than 10% of the cost as an advance before a written agreement for sale is registered
5. Look for a payment schedule linked to clear construction stages, pay through traceable channels and keep every receipt

## Completion, possession and after

For a building that is finished or nearly so, ask whether the completion or occupancy certificate has been issued by the authority concerned, confirming that the building has been completed in line with its approval. Check that water, sewerage and electricity connections are in place or have been applied for, and ask how common areas will be handed over to the owners' association and how maintenance will be organised.

Under RERA, the promoter is liable to fix structural defects or workmanship defects brought to their notice within five years of handing over possession. Keep a clear record of your possession date and report any defects in writing.

## On the ground: site visits and flood history

Documents tell one part of the story; the site and its surroundings tell the rest.

1. Visit at different times of day, including peak traffic hours
2. If you can, visit during or just after heavy rain in the northeast monsoon, roughly October to December
3. Ask long-standing residents and shopkeepers how the street fared during the floods of late 2015 and Cyclone Michaung in December 2023
4. Note the level of the plot and the building's plinth relative to the road, and any nearby lakes, canals or low-lying land
5. Check the access road, water source, drainage and the rainwater harvesting structure, which has been mandatory for buildings in Tamil Nadu since 2003
6. Walk the neighbourhood to judge schools, shops, transport and noise

Our article on [designing for Chennai's climate](/blogs/designing-for-chennai-climate) explains what to look for in the building itself, from plinth levels to waterproofing.

## Bring in a property lawyer

A qualified property lawyer is not an optional extra. Ask them to review the parent documents, patta, EC, approvals, the agreement for sale and the draft sale deed before you sign anything or make significant payments. A lawyer can also carry out checks that go beyond what is available online and advise on the specific risks of the property you are considering.

> The time to ask difficult questions is before you sign, not after you move in.
